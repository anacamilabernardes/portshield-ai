2. A schema-valid response with three substantive errors

Record: SYN-V2R-0001 · V2 adapter · development validation

Input report

The 06:00 bulletin attributed a halt in vessel docking to a tug fault. At 07:00 the duty officer withdrew that attribution: inspection found the tug sound, and instrument readings confirmed a lightning exclusion remains the sole reason docking is stopped. Classify the situation at 07:00. Road access is not covered.

Investigating the error

  1. Scope: docking is explicitly stopped. The affected function should be vessel_manoeuvring, rather than none.
  2. Status: “docking is stopped” supports suspended. V2 returns partial_restriction.
  3. Equipment evidence: the updated inspection found the tug sound. The label is false; null would mean unknown.

The existing adapter already got status and equipment right here. V2 corrected the event type but regressed on those two fields, while affected function remained incorrect. Schema validation accepts all these values: it cannot determine whether the narrative supports them.

Evidence, not a causal claim: the saved text and responses establish the observed mismatches. They do not prove why the model produced them. Investigate temporal updates, field scope and explicit negation in additional reviewed development examples.

3. Why one successful example is not enough

Across all 12 validation examples, affected-function errors stayed at 8; road-access errors fell from 5 to 3; operational-status errors rose from 5 to 7; fully correct records increased from 0 to 1. The successful case above is that single V2 exact match, deliberately paired with a known failure for explanation.

Decision: do not promote the V2 adapter. These are synthetic development cases, not an independent final test. Neither adapter has demonstrated operational readiness.
